Markets & Investors

Do Fund Managers Let Their Politics Pick Their Stocks?

Mutual fund managers tend to tilt their portfolios toward companies and industries that align with their own political leanings, and leaning into that bias can come at a cost to returns.

with Y. Xi · Journal of Financial & Quantitative Analysis · 2020 · Covered by the WSJ, CNBC & Reuters

Corporate Governance

Do Boards Really "Cause" Better Performance?

Governance and performance evolve together over time. Once that feedback is taken into account, many apparent effects of board structure on firm value no longer hold, a caution that reshaped how the field studies governance.

with J. Linck & J. Netter · Journal of Financial Economics · 2012
Boards & Directors

How Information Travels Through Boardroom Networks

When a firm's directors sit at the hub of a well-connected web of boards, informed trading in that firm's stock rises around major news, suggesting private information moves along director networks.

with F. Akbas & F. Meschke · Journal of Accounting & Economics · 2016
Insider Trading

The Quiet Channels of Insider Trading

Insiders can act on private information in indirect ways that standard surveillance tends to miss, a blind spot with real implications for how trading is monitored and enforced.

with B. Goldie, C. Jiang & P. Koch · Journal of Financial & Quantitative Analysis · 2023
Executives & Firms

When the Marketing Chief's Pay Package Misses the Mark

CMOs at advertising- and R&D-intensive firms command more pay, with more of it tied to the stock. And when a CMO's pay strays from what the role predicts, operating performance, earnings, and stock returns all suffer.

with N. Bansal, K. Joseph & M. Ma · Management Science · 2017
Real Estate & Housing

What Online Searches Reveal About Home Prices

Local internet search activity carries a genuine signal about where housing prices are headed, an early demonstration of using digital footprints to forecast a market.

with E. Beracha · Journal of Real Estate Research · 2013

Why Innovative Firms Sit on So Much Cash

Research-intensive firms hold larger cash reserves to weather the uncertainty and uneven timing of innovation spending, tying a company's cash policy to its appetite for R&D.

with Z. He · Journal of Corporate Finance · 2016
Boards & Directors

What a Director's Tax-Haven Ties Do to Corporate Taxes

Companies that add directors connected to island tax havens go on to adopt more aggressive tax strategies, showing how a single board appointment can reshape financial policy.

with C. Jiang, T. Kubick & M. Miletkov · Management Science · 2018
Insider Trading

Does Legal Know-How Change How Executives Trade?

Executives with legal expertise appear to time and structure their own-firm trades differently, shaping how, and how safely, they profit from what they know.

with C. Jiang & Y. Xi · Journal of Banking & Finance · 2021
Executives & Firms

What Deflates an Overconfident CEO?

Seeing a connected CEO fired unexpectedly deflates a chief executive's own overconfidence. In the year that follows, they make fewer acquisitions, and better ones, evidence that hubris is not a fixed trait but one that events can reset.

with Y. Xi · Journal of Empirical Finance · 2026

How Skilled Immigration Moves Home Prices

Using the randomness of the H-1B visa lottery to separate cause from correlation, the study traces how inflows of highly skilled immigrants affect local housing prices.

with E. Beracha, E. Kim & Y. Xi · Journal of Real Estate Research · in press
Markets & Innovation

How Small Data Choices Reshape M&A Findings

Drawing on nearly two decades of deals, the paper shows that routine filters applied to merger samples can meaningfully change what researchers conclude, a caution now widely cited.

with J. Netter & M. Stegemoller · Review of Financial Studies · 2011
Corporate Governance

When a "Friendly" Board Is a Feature, Not a Flaw

After Sarbanes-Oxley forced boards to add outsiders, many firms picked independent directors with ties to the CEO, especially where monitoring is costliest. Performance didn't suffer, suggesting friendly directors were an efficient way to absorb the cost of the new rules.

with Y. Xi · Journal of Corporate Finance · 2019
Boards & Directors

Importing Oversight from Abroad

Firms are more likely to recruit foreign independent directors from countries with strong legal institutions, in effect importing governance quality across borders.

with M. Miletkov & A. Poulsen · Journal of International Business Studies · 2017
Executives & Firms

Who Really Gains When Noncompetes Weaken?

When states make noncompetes harder to enforce, knowledge-intensive firms become more profitable and productive. The biggest gains go to the strongest firms, which attract the best talent once workers are free to move, widening the gap between leaders and laggards.

with Z. He · Journal of Economics & Management Strategy · in press
